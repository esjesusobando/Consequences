# LinkedIn Post: Molly Graham on Grief, Burnout and the AI Transition

## Introduction

Hi everyone. I wanted to share an analysis I recently did of Molly Graham's talk on burnout, grief and the AI organizational transition. The insights deeply impacted me and I wanted to weave them with our "compound engineering" approach — the idea that each unit of work should make the next easier, not harder.

## Main Body

### The LEGO Metaphor (and why it's not so simple anymore)

Molly Graham reminds us that "LEGOs" are the work we do, the tasks we identify with. The original advice: "Give away your Legos" — delegate to grow. But today the situation is more nuanced:

> "Not all LEGOs should be given away. Some LEGOs are essential and should not be given to AI."

Burnout has gone from 44% (2025) to 55% (2026) — a 10-point increase in one year. This makes sense: more work with fewer resources. The speed of technological change is relentless — 6 months can completely change the landscape.

### Grief is a Natural Part of Change

> "Grief is really real. It's not a sign that something is wrong."

Loneliness is an important aspect. Smaller teams and smaller companies are happier. In larger companies: leadership levels are eliminated, more pressure on productivity, less space for joy.

### Zentaur vs. Reverse Zentaur

Two powerful metaphors:

- **Favorable Zentaur**: Human body controlling an animal body (IA that we make do what we want)
- **Reverse Zentaur (fear)**: Animal body controlling the human (IA that uses us, like DoorDash, Uber)

The fear: "I'm just a wrapper that bridges gaps that humans still value, while the IA tells me where to build and what to do, and then she does the thing itself."

### The Learning Principle

> "What you can learn tomorrow is much more important than what you know today."

In the AI era: "What we knew 6 months ago can be completely out of date." Six months can completely change the landscape.

## Connection with Our Compound Engineering Philosophy

Here's where this becomes practical for us:

**Every insight we codify from this framework makes the next one easier to absorb, categorize and apply.** The principle of "each unit of work should make the subsequent unit easier, not harder" applies to both code and human development.

**What we're doing with this:**

1. **Codify insights** as reusable knowledge (they're in our vault under 03_Resources/)
2. **Map LEGOs to PARA zones** — what projects, areas, resources and archive each insight involves
3. **Adjust our 80/20 planning/execution ratio** for AI's change pace (knowledge half-life is ~6 months now)
4. **Validate emotions before optimizing** — you can't delegate/automate without first validating the emotional state

### Next Steps

- Audit what LEGOs we're delegating vs. retaining
- Restructure teams toward smaller groups when possible
- Establish formal learning rate that outpaces change velocity
- Validate emotion before any technical solution
- Document whether our agent operates as zentaur (controlling AI) or reverse zentaur (being controlled by AI)
